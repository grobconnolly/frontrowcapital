The fine print

Illustration only. Illustrative scenario calculator only — not a forecast, not an offer to sell securities, and not investment advice. An investor can lose all invested capital.

Same players, same pay. The average career earnings you select is applied to the earnings rights each side actually owns. Picking skill is not modeled; the same average career applies to both. For reference, the FanGraphs top-100 lists of 2005–2016 averaged $58M in career earnings when each season's salary is inflation-adjusted to 2026 dollars — consistent with the 2026 contract pricing used here; a $58M career returns $2.32M on a $300K contract before any fees.

Why the fund pays $300K for what costs $375K on Finlete. Player economics differ by design: the average Finlete deal acquires 4% of a career for $375K, while the average Fund I deal acquires 4% for $300K — a fund writing one up-front check negotiates better terms than capital raised deal-by-deal.

Terms. Finlete: 25% front-end markup on the check, 8.5% deal maker fee on the remainder, 10% back-end take on gross collections — on top of the higher per-deal price above. Fund I: management fees of 2% of committed capital in each of the first three years; of deployed capital, 15% buys the fund's Finlete equity stake and 85% signs athletes; 20% carry on gains above the athlete-deployed basis. Finlete stake proceeds are included in the Front Row figures at the outcome you select (0× by default), with carry computed per sleeve — 20% of each sleeve's gain above its own basis. Fund terms per the term sheet and operating agreement; Finlete pricing per current platform terms.

Not counted here. Diversification is not modeled — ten picks ride ten careers while Fund I spreads ~30 top-100 prospects plus the Finlete stake; for what that difference does to outcomes, run the full backtest simulator.
